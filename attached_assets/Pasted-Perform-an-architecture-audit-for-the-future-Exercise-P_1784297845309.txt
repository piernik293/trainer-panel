Long-term goals that the future engine must support:

1. Prefer appropriate compound exercises before isolation exercises.
2. Place heavy, technically demanding, high-fatigue exercises near the beginning of a session.
3. Place lower-demand exercises with shorter rest periods, such as many core, calf, and small isolation movements, near the end of a session.
4. Newly generated exercises must be inserted into the correct training position instead of simply being appended.
5. Exercise order should generally follow:
   - primary compound / main movement
   - secondary compound
   - accessory movement
   - isolation
   - core / low-fatigue finisher
6. Priority muscle groups may be placed at the beginning of a relevant session, but priority must not override exercise safety or sensible compound-first ordering.
7. Auto Fill must not consider a plan successful when an important required target remains completely uncovered, for example quadriceps at 0.
8. The generator should eventually optimize the whole training week, not only individual sessions.
9. It should avoid unnecessary fragmentation such as assigning the same exercise for 2 sets on two different days when one 4-set exposure would be more useful.
10. Higher frequency should remain possible when it has a real programming purpose, such as skill practice, main lifts, fatigue management, or improved session quality.
11. The future system may need concepts such as:
   - movement pattern
   - compound versus isolation
   - exercise priority tier
   - fatigue or demand level
   - technical difficulty
   - preferred session position
   - preferred weekly frequency
   - minimum useful sets per session
   - maximum useful sets per session
   - whether volume may be merged between sessions
12. The final goal is trainer-like decision making, not only maximizing volume coverage points.
