--------------------------------------------------
Goal
--------------------------------------------------

Stage 5 must remain a pure fragmentation tie-breaker.

It must not understand or interpret the internal structure of ScoreTuple.

The existing trainer logic should determine whether expansion has a meaningful advantage.

Stage 5 should only receive the final boolean result:

- expansionHasClearAdvantage = true
- expansionHasClearAdvantage = false

--------------------------------------------------
1. Introduce an advantage predicate
--------------------------------------------------

Design a small pure helper owned by the existing selection logic, not by Stage 5:

hasClearExpansionAdvantage(
  expansionCandidate,
  consolidationCandidate
): boolean

This helper may inspect the existing evaluation outcomes required to compare the two actions.

It should answer only:

“Does adding the new exercise provide a concrete benefit that consolidation cannot provide?”

Examples of a concrete advantage:

- expansion resolves more remaining targets
- expansion adds a movement category not currently represented
- another already-existing trainer rule clearly requires expansion

Do not add new scores, weights, thresholds, or heuristics.

Do not use category-count thresholds such as:

- penalty >= 2
- two exercises are enough
- three exercises are redundant

--------------------------------------------------
2. Keep Stage 5 independent
--------------------------------------------------

Stage 5 action resolution should not inspect ScoreTuple indices directly.

Use this conceptual interface:

resolveFragmentationAction({
  hasExpansion,
  hasConsolidation,
  expansionHasClearAdvantage
})

Decision rules:

1. no expansion and no consolidation
   → NO_ACTION

2. only expansion
   → EXPAND

3. only consolidation
   → CONSOLIDATE

4. both exist and expansionHasClearAdvantage is true
   → EXPAND

5. both exist and expansionHasClearAdvantage is false
   → CONSOLIDATE

This makes consolidation the final fallback only when expansion has no concrete advantage.
