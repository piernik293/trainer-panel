BALANCED PRIORITY REDUCTION
==================================================

Protect targets in this order:

1. priority 1,
2. priority 2,
3. priority 3,
4. non-priority targets.

Therefore, when reductions are necessary, reduce in this order:

1. non-priority targets,
2. priority 3,
3. priority 2,
4. priority 1 last.

Priority protection must be visible, but the remaining body must not become
severely underdeveloped.

Do not repeatedly reduce one muscle while comparable muscles remain at their
maximum.

Within the same priority group:

1. Reduce exactly one effective-volume point or the smallest supported step.
2. Calculate:
   current proposed volume / maxWeeklyVolume.
3. Select a target currently using the highest percentage of its maximum.
4. Recalculate after every reduction.
5. Rotate reductions across comparable targets.
6. Preserve the existing recommended minimum whenever reasonably possible.

Do not produce results such as:

- priority target at 100%,
- one non-priority muscle at 30%,
- all other non-priority muscles at 100%

unless a real health, equipment, exercise-availability or structural conflict
makes this unavoidable.

priority rank.

Do not flatten one user priority into several different priority positions.

PLAN COMPLETION
==================================================

After the complete base week has been generated and the existing whole-week
balancing pass has run, execute one server-side completion pass.

Do not implement the completion algorithm in React.

The completion pass must:

1. Calculate realised effective weekly volume for every target.
2. Compare realised volume with the final proposal.
3. Calculate the estimated duration of every workout day.
4. Detect meaningful remaining deficits.
5. Continue adding useful work when:
   - meaningful deficits remain,
   - session time remains,
   - max/session allows it,
   - recovery allows it,
   - health and equipment restrictions allow it.

Completion order:

1. priority 1,
2. priority 2,
3. priority 3,
4. non-priority target with the largest proportional deficit.

For every completion step:

1. First consider adding one working set to a suitable existing exercise.
2. Recalculate effective volume and duration.
3. Respect max/session.
4. When another exercise is needed, select a compatible exercise that adds:
   - a different movement direction,
   - a different muscle function,
   - a different resistance profile,
   - or necessary direct work.
5. Do not add a nearly identical exercise variant without a clear reason.
6. Recalculate the complete week after every addition.
7. Recalculate the affected day duration after every addition.
8. Preserve:
   - health restrictions,
   - avoided exercises,
   - equipment restrictions,
   - required movement patterns.

Multiple compound exercises may coexist in one session when they have
meaningfully different functions.

Valid example:

- squat,
- hip hinge,
- hip thrust.

Do not create a rigid rule allowing only one compound movement family per day.

A selected session duration is a real client preference.

Example:

When an Advanced client selected 90 minutes and an Upper session currently lasts
approximately 47 minutes while important volume deficits remain, the generator
must continue adding useful work when safe and feasible.

Do not require every workout to last exactly the selected time.

Do not add random exercises only to fill minutes.

Stop completion when:

- all final proposals are satisfied,
- the next legal addition would exceed sessionDurationMinutes,
- max/session would be exceeded,
- max/week or the allowed priority extension would be exceeded,
- recovery or restrictions prevent additional work,
- no safe compatible exercise remains.

CONTROLLED PRIORITY EXTENSION
==================================================

The normal max/week remains the standard target.

In exceptional cases the generator may exceed normal max/week only for priority
muscles.

Allowed weekly extension:

- priority 1: up to 10% above normal max/week,
- priority 2: up to 10% above normal max/week,
- priority 3: up to 5% above normal max/week.

Do not round the internal extended ceiling to a whole number.

Effective-volume values may remain decimal.

The UI may display one or two decimal places.

Automatic priority extension is allowed only when all conditions are met:

- experience level is Intermediate or Advanced,
- training goal is physique, hypertrophy, recomposition or another compatible
  muscle-development goal,
- recovery evaluation permits additional volume,
- trainingDaysPerWeek allows the work to be distributed sensibly,
- every affected day remains within sessionDurationMinutes,
- max/session remains respected,
- no relevant health restriction prevents additional work,
- required movement patterns remain preserved,
- non-priority muscles have reached their final adjusted proposals,
- the additional work does not require unnecessary near-duplicate exercises.

Do not use automatic priority extension for:

- Brak aktywności fizycznej,
- Początkujący.

The generator must first satisfy the normal max/week proposal.

Only after normal proposals are satisfied may the completion pass consider
controlled priority extension.

Examples:

Priority 1:

“Maksymalna rekomendowana objętość: 20 pkt.
Rozszerzenie dla priorytetu 1: +2 pkt.
Końcowa propozycja: 22 pkt.”

Priority 2:

“Maksymalna rekomendowana objętość: 20 pkt.
Rozszerzenie dla priorytetu 2: +2 pkt.
Końcowa propozycja: 22 pkt.”

Priority 3:

“Maksymalna rekomendowana objętość: 20 pkt.
Rozszerzenie dla priorytetu 3: +1 pkt.
Końcowa propozycja: 21 pkt.”

The existing whole-week balancer must treat a valid calculated priority
extension as permitted.

It must not reduce a valid priority extension back to normal max/week.

Anything above the calculated extended ceiling must never be generated
automatically.
